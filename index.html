Root Cause Analysis: Supervisory & Process Gaps

The direct equipment failure was preventable. The investigation revealed that the incident stemmed from three key process breakdowns. Click each tab below to explore the supervisory gaps that contributed to the failure.

Absence of Tier-1 Critical Spares

No critical hydraulic spares (hoses, seals, sensors) were available on the rig site. All spares were located at the supply base.

Result: Significantly delayed troubleshooting and repair time, guaranteeing NPT.

Pre-Job Checklist (PJC) Inaccuracy

The PJC for the HPU was signed off by the operator without physical verification or a proper functional pressure test.

Result: Worn components and failing sensors were missed during pre-job checks.

Fatigue Management Failure

The HPU operator was working on an extended shift due to a crew shortage, compromising alertness during maintenance checks.

Result: Human error compounded the equipment risk, leading to a failure to spot the issue.

Critical Supervisor Intervention

Upon notification of the failure, the supervisor's response was focused on three immediate priorities: mitigating well integrity risk, mobilizing resources, and managing stakeholders.

Step 1

Prioritize Well Integrity

Issued an urgent directive to the drilling team to conduct periodic (30-min interval) mud circulation sweeps to prevent the static casing from getting stuck.

Step 2

Mobilize Resources

Activated the Emergency Call-Out protocol for a fully certified backup HPU unit and a specialized hydraulic technician from the supply base.

Step 3

Manage Stakeholders

Established an hourly status report to the Company Man and Rig Manager, providing transparent NPT projections (Worst-Case vs. Best-Case).

Lessons Learned & The Way Forward

The key takeaway is that a significant six-figure NPT cost resulted from a small, preventable spare part failure. The following supervisor-driven recommendations were proposed to build a more resilient TRS operation.

Goal: Ensure true integrity of Pre-Job Checklists.
Action: Implement a policy where the Technician *and* the Supervisor must co-sign on critical functional tests (e.g., pressure tests, sensor checks) before the job.
Goal: Eliminate NPT caused by missing inventory.
Action: Create a standardized **Tier-1 Critical Spares Kit** that must be physically audited and verified on the rig site *before* commencing any TRS operation.
Goal: Mitigate human error in safety-critical tasks.
Action: Use digital tracking to enforce maximum working hours for specialized personnel. Implement a mandatory 6-hour rest rule before any safety-critical function.
Goal: Increase team resilience and flexibility.
Action: Cross-train at least two operators per shift on basic troubleshooting for critical equipment (e.g., HPU, Tongs) to reduce reliance on a single specialist.
